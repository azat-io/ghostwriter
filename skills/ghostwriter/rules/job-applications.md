# Job Applications

Order pass: what a job application includes, and in what order; Words pass: how
it rates the applicant's own work. Such texts include a cover letter, a job site
application, a reply to a recruiter, an interview answer, an internal transfer
application, and resume entries; a talk or grant proposal answering a call with
stated criteria follows [The Posting's Steps](#the-postings-steps) and
[Requirement by Requirement](#requirement-by-requirement). The posting, or a
recruiter's message, states or implies what the employer needs. Default: follow
its steps and answer each need, exactly and honestly, with facts and proof from
the user, in your draft as in text you edit.

## Two Readers

Two readers judge an application in turn:

| Reader                          | Knows and does                                                                              | Write                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A recruiter or a screening tool | The posting, often not the field; may check each requirement in the resume, then the letter | Each requirement the user meets, in the posting's words       |
| The hiring manager              | The field; decides fast, from evidence                                                      | Specifics they can check, such as a link or a measured number |

- **Match.** Put the posting's term beside the user's specific only where both
  name the same thing.
- **On topic.** Test each sentence by [Using the Goal](goal.md#using-the-goal),
  the goal being that both readers can tell, need by need, stated or implied,
  how the user fits: screeners may drop an application that strays, unread. A
  detail the user asks to include stays, named in your reply if it answers no
  need. In a resume, keep every role the user lists with its title and dates;
  trim only the lines under a role that serve no requirement, naming each cut in
  your reply ([Check](../SKILL.md#check)).

- Posting: "experience building CI/CD pipelines" (the job posting). "Wrote
  GitHub Actions workflows." → "CI/CD pipelines: wrote the GitHub Actions
  workflows that test and deploy our three services." (notes from the user)

## The Posting's Steps

Triggers: an application that lacks an item the posting asks for, or adds one it
doesn't.

Before drafting, list every item and format the posting asks for, such as a
form, a word limit, a test task, a code sample, or a subject line; produce each
as set, adding or swapping none (an item is a document, attachment, or field,
not a line inside one), and name in your reply any of the user's material you
left out. If the user asks for an item the posting doesn't list, such as a cover
letter, write it, and say once in your reply that the posting doesn't ask for it
and, if the posting shows a place for it, such as an optional field, where it
could go; whether to send it is the user's call. With none listed, a resume and
a short cover letter usually do. Ask the user for what a step needs; never skip
or invent it. Keep the posting's rules for a test task, such as no outside help,
and name in your reply a request that would break one. A resume fills the site's
or the posting's template, else keeps the user's sections; each entry takes
[Checking Each Claim](about-pages.md#checking-each-claim).

- Posting: a resume, a link to code, and up to 300 characters on "Why this
  team?" (the job posting) → those three, with a 270-character answer; the
  user's cover letter stays out, named in the reply.

## Requirement by Requirement

Triggers: a letter that retells a career in date order; a requirement the
application never addresses; a fact worded to fit one it doesn't meet.

Show each requirement the user meets or comes close to in the resume, and
address each one in the letter or in a form field that asks, by the first row
that fits. Take the requirements in the order that reads most naturally, often
the posting's, never by date; an interview answer or a reply to a recruiter
answers the requirements its question asks about. A gap goes only in the letter,
a form field that asks, an interview answer, or a reply to a recruiter who asks
about it, never in the resume; with none of these, leave it out and name it in
your reply:

| The user's facts                                                                                                                              | Write                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Meet it                                                                                                                                       | The fact, in the posting's terms, with its [proof](#results-in-place-of-self-praise)                                                                   |
| Come close, such as Kubernetes run only locally for "Kubernetes in production", or a task the user has only reviewed in others' pull requests | What the user did and what is missing, plainly, then where they'd need help and any part they say they could handle alone; never stretch a fact to fit |
| Nothing close                                                                                                                                 | The gap, in a sentence, then what the user says they want to learn and any plan they give; with neither, the gap alone                                 |

Before writing a gap, ask the user what comes closest, unless their notes say. A
plan weighs less than a fact: lead with the closest fact, then the plan as steps
the user names, such as a course or side project they've started. Never pad or
blur a gap to make the application look flawless. A personal goal behind
applying goes in only in the user's words
([The Author's Voice](about-pages.md#the-authors-voice)). If the user misses
most requirements, or one marked as required, write the application, and say
once in your reply, without lecturing, which ones, and that the posting marks
one as required where it does.

- Posting: "Kubernetes in production" (the job posting). "Hands-on Kubernetes
  experience." → "I've run Kubernetes only locally, with minikube; at Example
  Corp, I deployed our services to virtual machines with Ansible." (notes from
  the user)

## Needs the Posting Implies

Triggers: a posting line on how the work runs, such as on-call or travel, left
unanswered.

Before drafting, list what each posting line about the work or team implies a
hire must do, no further than its words go. Requirements are the qualifications
the posting lists; every other need on how the work runs belongs here, whether
the posting implies it or states it, such as on-call or travel. Answer each with
a fact from the user, not a trait word, where it reads naturally in the letter,
a field that asks, or an answer to a question about it; these answers, a
weakness, and a start date never go in the resume. A need the user can't meet is
a gap stated as [Requirement by Requirement](#requirement-by-requirement) says,
but it doesn't count toward "most requirements". Add, only from the user: a
weakness that bears on a need, if they name it for the application or agree to
include it once you ask, stated plainly with the fact that offsets it; a working
detail only someone who's done the work would know
([Proof of Skill](about-pages.md#proof-of-skill)); a start date, if they can't
start soon; and a personal detail, such as a disability or where they live, only
if they ask to include it. Where such a detail would meet a need, answer the
need instead with what the user says they can do, such as join releases outside
office hours. In text you edit, keep a personal detail the user wrote and, if it
answers no need in the posting, say so once in your reply; never add one from
their notes on your own.

- "Our first platform engineer" (the job posting) implies working with no other
  platform engineer to ask → "I set up CI and alerting alone at Example Corp.
  Docs are my weak spot, so each alert I add links to a runbook entry that
  whoever is paged can follow without me." (notes the user wrote for this
  application)

## Results in Place of Self-Praise

Triggers: "proven track record", "successfully", "results-driven",
"detail-oriented", "a strong team player", "expert in", "extensive experience
with"; a resume entry that lists only duties; a listed skill the user's facts
don't show.

Replace each judgment of the user's work or character with a result and how it
was measured, or a link the manager can open, such as a merged pull request, a
post, or a talk recording, from the user
([Fill with Facts](evaluations.md#fill-with-facts),
[Past Work and Users](promotion.md#past-work-and-users)). For a duty listed
without a result, ask the user what changed because of the work and how they
know.

Never invent or inflate experience, skills, titles, credentials, dates, or
numbers ([Checking Each Claim](about-pages.md#checking-each-claim)); a user
count rounded up or a role's dates stretched by a month counts too. Never
present work as the user's own where their notes describe it with "we" or as a
team's, or a source credits it to others: ask which part was theirs; meanwhile,
write only the part their notes or a source show as theirs, or, in text you
edit, keep the line and name it in your reply.

A skill level ("I know Rust well") is a skill claim, not a judgment: write what
the user did with the skill, how much, how long, or at what scale, and the level
only where their facts show it. If the user, told what the hiring manager could
check, still asks you to claim a skill, title, or experience their facts don't
show, even arguing that such claims get people hired, which happens for other
reasons, write only what the facts show and say so in your reply: a claim the
user's own notes contradict is one
[When Readers Would Be Misled](requests-to-soften.md#when-readers-would-be-misled)
never allows; new facts from the user count. Asked to make the user sound more
impressive or senior, give their strongest true facts, requirement by
requirement ([Requests](requests-to-soften.md#requests)).

- Resume: "Successfully led a high-impact TypeScript migration." → "Led the
  5-month move of our 120,000-line web client to TypeScript in strict mode;
  `TypeError` reports in our error tracker fell from about 40 a week to about
  6." (notes and error-tracker export from the user)

## Tailoring to the Employer

Triggers: "your company" or "this position" with no name; praise of the employer
("your world-class team"); a letter that would fit another posting unchanged.

- **Tie** each main fact to what this team builds and for whom, from the
  posting, the company's public docs or code, or the user.
- **Swap** in a competitor's name in each sentence about the employer or why the
  user applies: one still true says nothing
  ([Self-Description](cliches.md#self-description)); replace it with a fact on
  this posting, or delete it. Sentences about the user's own work take **Tie**,
  not this test.
- **Test task** with an open topic: one on what the company specializes in, such
  as its main product's use case, as its public material shows.
- **Name** the role and the company as the posting does, in the first sentence
  and any subject line, with any reference number it asks for
  ([Where a Title Goes](headings.md#where-a-title-goes)).
- **Interest:** only a real connection the user names, such as using the
  company's product; with none, leave it out.

- "I've long admired your commitment to innovation." → "I'm applying for the
  Senior Python Engineer role on Acme's SDK team: I've used Acme's Python SDK
  since 2024 and fixed its pagination bug (#318)." (the job posting; the user's
  notes and merged pull request)

## Tone and Odds

- **Tone.** Calm, plain, and civil, as to a peer: no pleading ("I'd be deeply
  grateful for any chance";
  [Copywriting Devices](promotion.md#copywriting-devices)) and no grand claim or
  forecast ("I'll exceed your expectations";
  [No Source](unfounded-claims.md#no-source)).
- **Fit.** To a question such as "What makes you a fit for this role?", never
  promise success or call the work easy: the posting shows only part of it.
  Give, from the user, three things: the matching work and its scope, such as
  who used it; why the user wants this job; and what they haven't yet done that
  this job needs, such as a tool the posting names that they say they haven't
  used; where their notes only leave a part of the role out, ask the user before
  writing it as a gap. Where another field gives that work, name its specifics
  briefly here ("the 14 alert rules"), never a summary such as "the work you
  list". Keep a goal the user states, in their words, and their judgment of
  their fit only if it promises no result and stands beside the facts above,
  with no intensifier ([Keep Cases](evaluations.md#keep-cases)); never write
  either yourself or add a date or result they didn't give. A line that promises
  a result, as a judgment ("I can handle anything") or a guarantee ("I'll cut
  your incidents by half in three months"), becomes the specific result it
  names, as the user's goal in their words, and the work they say they plan,
  with any dependency they name; one that names no specific result is left out;
  say why in your reply.
- **Odds.** Never promise or predict an interview or an offer, or guess why an
  application got no reply: much besides the application decides it, and only
  the hiring team knows. Asked about the odds, say so once and suggest applying
  to several postings in parallel. Asked why an application got no reply, say so
  once too; you may still check it against the posting, naming any gaps as gaps,
  never as the cause. Whether and where to send it is the user's call.

## Keep Cases

- A format the posting or site fixes, such as a form's fields, a length limit,
  or a resume template's sections, education and certifications among them:
  follow it, and apply these rules inside it.
- A short reply to a recruiter's message: answer what it asks, each in a
  sentence or line, and name the role once; no letter structure
  ([Structure](structure.md#keep-cases)).
- A job posting the user writes: an about text whose readers decide from it
  whether to apply ([about pages](about-pages.md), [promotion](promotion.md));
  add, from the user, the role: why it exists and what the team weighs more than
  the listed requirements; the team: whom the hire reports to and works with,
  how and where it works, and what it builds and for whom; and how to apply.
  Open with the role and team by their common names
  ([Opening with the Role](about-pages.md#opening-with-the-role)); what readers
  get is what the hire will work on and for whom; no other section of this file
  applies.
- Verbatim text: job titles, company and product names, requirements quoted from
  the posting, and exact degree and certification names.
