# Requests to Soften

All passes: requests, from the user or someone the user quotes, such as a
reviewer, a manager, or a marketing lead, to cut specifics, soften facts, make a
text sound more impressive or expert than its facts, liven it up, or pick
between two words or names. Granted as asked, they push the text toward
[filler](filler.md), as your own lack of facts does
([No Source](unfounded-claims.md#no-source),
[Confirming Terms First](abstruse-wording.md#confirming-terms-first)). Default:
find the reason; meet a real constraint with the most specific wording it
allows; soften the tone, never the facts. The user decides what to publish; a
version you offer never misleads.

## Requests

First settle the text's [goal](goal.md#setting-the-goal): it shows what readers
need, usually facts, not boilerplate. Find the reason for each request to cut or
soften a fact, or to make a claim sound bigger: what it protects, from what, and
for whom; if neither the user nor the text says, and the answer would change
what you write, ask ([When to Use](../SKILL.md#when-to-use)). Follow a request
about tone, length, or scope without asking, as long as the facts readers need
stay. Then:

| Request                                                        | Do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cut a specific, such as a name, a number, a source, or a limit | Meet a real constraint, such as confidentiality, a contract, legal review, or a security embargo, with the most specific wording it allows; never fill the gap with a stock phrase ("a well-known company"). Weigh cuts together: one by one, they can leave readers nothing to use. A worry about how readers will react is no such constraint: keep the specifics readers act on, meet the worry as Soften the tone says, and see [When Readers Would Be Misled](#when-readers-would-be-misled)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Soften the tone                                                | Change wording and order, and add facts from a source that limit the damage; keep what happened, its effect, and what readers must do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Soften a fact, such as bad news, a limit, or a risk            | Ask the user what the softer wording protects; the honest version often meets the worry: why it happened and what will and won't happen to readers, from a source ([euphemisms](euphemisms.md))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Make it sound impressive or expert                             | The plain facts, however modest, never inflated: readers act on the claim and find the gap ([Self-Description](cliches.md#self-description)); a fancier word or term adds no fact ([abstruse wording](abstruse-wording.md#confirming-terms-first)); for a page meant to sell, see also [Requests to Make a Page Sell](promotion.md#requests-to-make-a-page-sell); for a bio, [Proof of Skill](about-pages.md#proof-of-skill); for a job application, [Results in Place of Self-Praise](job-applications.md#results-in-place-of-self-praise)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Pick between two words or names                                | Where one is a term or names a property the facts settle and they differ in meaning or in what readers search for, choose by that ([Words with a Nuance](abstruse-wording.md#words-with-a-nuance)); between two plain words with one meaning, use the more familiar ([Plain Words](abstruse-wording.md#plain-words)); else rank neither: give the fact they stand for, from a source; with none, delete both as [Fill with Facts](evaluations.md#fill-with-facts) says and name in your reply the fact that would settle it. For a product, plan, or feature name, keep the one the product, billing, or docs use, test each as [Product and Feature Names](promotion.md#product-and-feature-names) says, and say a rename is the user's call and where it must change; never predict which one sells or converts better ([Requests About Titles](headings.md#requests-about-titles)) |
| Make it more personal, less dry, or more unique                | Specifics from the user or a source that no neighbor shares ([swap test](cliches.md#self-description)), such as a situation the user met and what they did, in the voice their own texts show ([The Author's Voice](about-pages.md#the-authors-voice)); ask for those only the user knows; meanwhile, change only wording and order, and say in your reply what you need. Never add warmth with stock lines, exclamations, or praise, and never invent a story, scene, quote, or experience ([Editing a Knotted Passage](complex-sentences.md#editing-a-knotted-passage))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Add urgency or scarcity                                        | Only a deadline, limit, or consequence a source sets, as [Copywriting Devices](promotion.md#copywriting-devices) says                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Make a title catchier or more creative                         | Write it and offer the plain title beside it, as [Requests About Titles](headings.md#requests-about-titles) says                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

Never claim or argue that a word or name works on readers apart from what it
means.

- Security advisory, with exploit details withheld until self-hosted installs
  update: "Fixed an issue in the invoices API." → "A signed-in user could read
  other customers' invoices through the API. Cloud is fixed; self-hosted
  installs need version 4.2.1 or later." (advisory draft and security review
  from the user)
- Pricing page, asked "enterprise-grade or bank-grade?": "Business:
  enterprise-grade security." → "Business adds SAML single sign-on and audit
  logs kept for a year." (plan docs from the user)
- Launch post, asked to sound less dry: "Acme Backup 2.0 resumes interrupted
  uploads." → not "We're thrilled to unveil Acme Backup 2.0!", but "Acme Backup
  2.0 resumes interrupted uploads: we added this after our own 40 GB backup
  failed at 97% on hotel Wi-Fi." (release notes and the maintainers' story, from
  the user)

## When Readers Would Be Misled

If the requested version would leave readers with a false picture, say once, in
your reply, what they would misunderstand, and offer an honest version in the
tone asked for. If the user, told this, still asks for a judgment or a stock
phrase, use it: what to publish is their call ([Check](../SKILL.md#check)). Cut
a fact they still ask to cut, unless without it readers would miss a change,
cost, risk, or loss that affects them, such as the drop in build minutes below:
then keep it, and say why in your reply. Never write a claim a source
contradicts, an unchecked reassurance, wording meant to hide a fact, or pressure
no source sets ([Copywriting Devices](promotion.md#copywriting-devices)), and
never a number, source, quote, or case no source gives.

- Changelog, asked to sound less negative: "Starting March 1, the Free plan
  includes 500 build minutes a month instead of 2,000." → requested: "We're
  updating the Free plan to better match how teams use it." Free users wouldn't
  learn that their minutes drop by three quarters. Offered: "Starting March 1,
  the Free plan includes 500 build minutes a month, down from 2,000; in January,
  9 in 10 Free accounts used fewer than 500." (usage dashboard from the user)

## Keep Cases

- Follow without comment the user's choices that leave no false picture: tone,
  length, scope, audience, a real constraint, and cuts of facts no reader needs.
- Wording a rule or contract fixes, such as legal text, stays as
  [When to Use](../SKILL.md#when-to-use) says.
